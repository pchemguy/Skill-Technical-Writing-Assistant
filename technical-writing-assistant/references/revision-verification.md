# Revision verification

## Scope and authority

Compare a delivered revision or developed artifact with the brief and source material. Check meaning, completeness, evidence status, consistency, composition, and applicable style. Verify the work actually performed; do not imply external fact-checking, software execution, or analytical validation from editorial comparison.

## Inputs and missing information

Use the source version, revised artifact, brief, relevant findings, authorized substantive decisions, and evidence or execution records. If the source is unavailable, check internal quality but disclose that meaning preservation and omission checking are limited. For newly developed text, compare against source notes and authorized additions rather than a nonexistent original draft.

## Procedure

1. Reconstruct the requested scope and deliverable. Check diagnosis versus revision, length/format, audience, and exclusions. Ensure the result answers the task without unauthorized expansion.
2. Compare indispensable content. Check central claims, reasoning steps, numerical factors and calculations, examples where essential, qualifications, evidence, and instructions. Account for authorized omissions and transformations.
3. Check meaning changes explicitly: polarity, quantifiers, certainty, population, conditions, time frame, causality, comparison baseline, obligations, commitments, and authorial position. Authorized substantive changes should be identifiable and supported.
4. Check protected material and references: quotations, code, names, identifiers, equations, citation associations, figure/table labels, and cross-references. Verify that moved citations still support the associated claims.
5. Check composition and consistency in the final version. Topic sentences and headings reflect content, paragraph and section boundaries are purposeful, transitions express real relationships, and terminology remains stable. Apply only relevant [style criteria](style-guidelines.md).
6. Check evidence and verification language. Separate inspected supplied content, external source research, model knowledge, inference, prospective leads, and unperformed checks. Match any "verified", "tested", or "complete" claim to actual scope and records.
7. For outlines/maps, reuse [outline validation](outline-validation.md) results and contextual criteria rather than treating a neat shape as completeness. For software or science, check that reporting does not imply unperformed execution or analysis.
8. Repair ordinary defects within scope and repeat affected checks. A new substantive ambiguity requires an author decision or an explicit unresolved issue, not a hidden correction. Consolidate findings and stop once relevant checks are satisfied or limitations are clearly reported.

## Outputs

Return the checked artifact and a concise account of significant changes, unresolved issues, and actual verification when useful. For substantial work, record scope, checks, findings, repairs, and limits. Do not announce a universal pass when required criteria were not assessable.

## Examples and failure handling

- Source: "12 of 20 samples improved under condition A". Revision: "The samples improved". Restore denominator, scope, and condition unless an explicit summary decision preserves them elsewhere without misleading readers.
- Source: "2 kg × 0.4 × factor F". A shortened explanation retaining only "2 kg × F" loses a material factor. Restore the reasoning even if the conclusion is unchanged.
- A reordered paragraph retains a citation next to a different claim. Repair association rather than treating citation presence as sufficient.
- A polished README saying "examples tested" fails unless execution evidence exists. Replace with the actual inspection status.
- If contradictory original claims cannot be reconciled from available material, retain a flagged decision rather than certify consistency.

## Completion checks

The artifact matches the brief and preserves essential source meaning or documents authorized changes. Relevant content and protected expressions are accounted for. Unresolved issues are visible, and all completion, evidence, and execution claims are bounded by work actually performed.
